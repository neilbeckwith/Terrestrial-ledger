The six processes — the science behind the numbers

Every tonne in the table below got into the land through photosynthesis, and whether it stays there is decided by the other five. Gt = 1 Pg.

What the numbers can and cannot say: no published source splits any region's net flux into process shares, so this page does not invent one. Each region's drivers are attributed qualitatively — which processes dominate there, and why — with the source cited. The hard numbers are the global ones: GPP 123 ± 8 PgC/yr, soil respiration 98 ± 12 PgC/yr, ~1,500 GtC of soil carbon in the top metre, ~1,300 PgC in permafrost-region soils.

Why 2024 was a weak year for the land

The year 2024 saw the largest atmospheric CO₂ growth rate on record, largely because the tropical land sink collapsed — the same pattern as the 2015–16 El Niño. The atmospheric inversions put the 2024 tropical land flux at a source of 1.2 ± 0.9 GtC/yr, against a 0.2 ± 0.7 GtC/yr sink in the DGVMs (Friedlingstein et al. 2025, §3.8.2). The two methods disagree on the size of the tropical collapse, but not on its cause: heat and drought cut photosynthesis and raised respiration and fire across the tropics, while the northern land sink held up better. The low-latency budget for 2025 shows the rebound — about 80% of the area where the sink had weakened showed some recovery, with tropical recovery exceeding 100% and the northern extratropics still incomplete (Ciais et al. 2026).

How to read the columns. Net flux, DGVM: the land's net uptake as simulated by the dynamic global vegetation models — natural sink minus land-use emissions. Net flux, inversions: the same quantity as seen from atmospheric CO₂ measurements — the independent check, and the two often disagree, which is itself information. Natural sink (SLAND): what the land would take up without land-use change. Land-use emissions (ELUC): what clearing forest and draining peat emits, from the bookkeeping models. Dominant processes: which of the six processes do most of the work there (see the processes section above).

Reconciliation: the ten regional DGVM net fluxes sum to 1.30 GtC/yr, against the global 2015–2024 mean net land flux (SLAND minus bookkeeping ELUC) of 0.99 GtC/yr. The gap is the DGVM underestimate of ELUC: in South America the bookkeeping models put ELUC at ~0.5 GtC/yr against ~0.3 in the DGVMs, and in Southeast Asia ~0.4 against ~0.2 (Friedlingstein et al. 2025, §3.8.2). Regional shares are therefore not computed against a single total on this page.

Over the longer run the land was a source, not a sink. From 1850 to 2024, cumulative land-use emissions (250 ± 65 GtC) exceeded the cumulative natural land sink (175 ± 50 GtC), making the global land a net source of 75 ± 80 GtC over the whole period (Friedlingstein et al. 2025, §3.9.1). The land only became a net sink in recent decades, as fossil-driven CO₂ fertilisation and regrowth began to outweigh clearing.

The variables that watch it — GCOS terrestrial Essential Climate Variables

The land sink is not measured by one instrument. It is constrained by this set of 20 terrestrial Essential Climate Variables (ECVs) — the minimum set needed to assess the land's state and change, coordinated by the Global Climate Observing System (GCOS). The observing framework is GTOS, the Global Terrestrial Observing System: its Terrestrial Observation Panel for Climate (TOPC) is sponsored jointly by GCOS, the FAO-led GTOS and the World Climate Research Programme (WCRP). Source: GCOS ECV list, gcos.wmo.int (accessed 2026-11-26); groups are GCOS's own.

VariableGCOS groupRole in the ledger

How to read the table. Hydrology and cryosphere variables set the water and the cold that the sink depends on; biology variables are the sink itself, watched from space and the ground; the two human-use variables are the pressure on it. Note: unlike the ocean ECVs, the terrestrial list has no pilot tier — all 20 are established ECVs. The current list adds terrestrial water storage, land surface temperature, evaporation from land and anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions to the older 16-variable terrestrial list, and renames water use as anthropogenic water use.

Which variables constrain the uptake?

The sink is pinned down first by FAPAR and leaf area index (how much light the canopy catches), land cover and above-ground biomass (what grows where, and how much carbon it holds), soil moisture and terrestrial water storage (the water that decides whether photosynthesis runs — land-flux anomalies track GRACE water storage), soil carbon (the stock itself), fire (the disturbance that returns carbon to the air), land surface temperature (heat stress), and permafrost and snow (the frozen store and its insulation). Each region's detail panel lists the ECVs that watch that region.

Methods, model assumptions & sources

What is counted

The headline global figure is the Global Carbon Budget land sink (SLAND): the net uptake of CO₂ by the land caused by rising atmospheric CO₂ and climate, in GtC per year. It is the average of 22 dynamic global vegetation models (S2 simulation), adjusted for land-cover bias (the "replaced sinks and sources" adjustment, a 19% reduction) and, for 2015–2024, a −0.07 GtC/yr adjustment for land-to-ocean fluxes the DGVMs do not simulate. Uncertainty is ±0.5 GtC/yr on average, and ±1.1 GtC/yr in 2024. The regional figures are the decadal-mean (2015–2024) RECCAP-2 region fluxes from the same paper's Figure 15 and §3.8.2.

What is not in the headline figure

SLAND excludes land-use-change emissions — they are shown separately as ELUC (1.25 GtC/yr in 2024) and subtracted to give the net land flux. The regional table does not include an annual series: no published annual regional land series exists, so the table is decadal means. The 2025 preliminary figure is a net land flux from a different, low-latency framework (3 DGVMs + OCO-2 inversions), not a GCB SLAND value, and is not added to the cumulative counter for that reason.

Model & estimate assumptions

  • Live counter: the 2024 land rate (7.12 GtCO₂/year) is spread evenly across the year — about 226 tonnes of CO₂ per second — and applied to 2026 until a newer annual total is published. It is an accounting spread, not a real-time measurement; real uptake varies by season, drought and El Niño, and in some years the land is a net source.
  • Cumulative live total: cumulative land uptake at the end of 2024 (118.1 GtC) plus the 2026 estimate from 1 January to now, at the same even rate. The preliminary 2025 net land flux is not added, because it is a net flux (sink minus land-use emissions), not SLAND.
  • Regional fluxes: decadal means for 2015–2024 from GCB 2025 Figure 15. Values stated in the paper text are used verbatim; values not stated in the text are read from the figure to the nearest 0.05 GtC/yr and flagged in data.json (value_source "fig"). DGVM and inversion net fluxes are independent estimates of the same quantity and often disagree — the disagreement is shown, not averaged away.
  • Process attribution: qualitative only. No published source splits a region's net flux into process shares; the page attributes dominant processes per region from the cited driver descriptions and says so where a split is not available.
  • Rounding: figures are shown to two decimals (GtC) or one decimal (GtC/yr) as shown; underlying values are in data.json.
